【2019a-jx · gaozhong·yingyu · 单选 · 第24题】

In what sense is the arbitrary classification of books considered to be impersonal？

- A. It brings about surprises
- B. It fails to track readers’ habits
- C. It ignores the content of books
- D. It fails to consider reader's preferences（二）请阅读 Passage2，完成第26-30小题。Passage 2 If you have got kids， here is a nasty truth： they are probably not very special， that is， they are average， ordinary， and unremarkable. Consider the numbers of those applications your daughter is sending to Ivy League schools， for instance. There are more than a quarter of a million other kids aiming for the same eight colleges at the same time， and less than 9% of them will make the cut And those hours you spend coaching Little League because you just know your sons sweet swing will take him to the professionals. There are 2. 4 million other Little Leaguers out there， and there are exactly 750 openings for major league ballplayers at the beginning of each season. That gives him a 0.0313% chance of reaching the big clubs. The odds are just as long for the other dreams you've had for your kids： your child the billionaire， the Broadway star， the Rhodes scholar. Most of those things are never going to happen The kids are paying the price for parents' delusions. In public schools， some students are bringing home 17.5 hours of homework per week or 3.5 per school night and it's hard to see how they have time to do it. From 2004 to 2014， the number of children participating in up to three hours of after-school activities on any given day rose from 6.5 million to 10.2 million. And all the while，the kids are being fed a promise-that they can be tutored and coached， pushed and tested， hot housed and advance placed until success is assured At last， a growing chorus of educators and psychologists is saying， “Enough！”Somewhere between the self-esteem building of going for the gold and the self esteem crushing of the Ivy-or-die ethos there has to be a place where kids can breathe， where they can have the freedom to do what they love and where parents accustomed to pushing their children to excel can shake off the newly defined shame of having raised an ordinary child. If the system is going to be fixed， it has to start， no surprise， with the parents. For them，the problem isn't merely the expense of the tutors， the chore of the homework checking and the constant search for just the right summer program. It's also the sweat equity that comes from agonizing over every exam， grieving over every disappointing grade-becoming less a guide in a child's academic career than an intimate fellow traveler. The first step for parents is accepting that they have less control over their children's education than they think they do a reality that can be both sobering and liberating. You can sign your kids up for ballet camp or violin immersion all you want， but if they're simply doing what they're told instead of doing what they love， they'll take it only so far. Ultimately， there's a much larger national conversation that needs to be had about just what higher education means and when it's needed at all. Four years of college has been sold as being a golden ticket in the American economy， and to an extent that's true. But pushing all kids down the bachelor's path ensures not only that some of them will lose their way but also that critical jobs that require a two-year or less-skilled trades， some kinds of nursing，computer technology， airline mechanics and more-will go unfilled There will never be a case to be made for a culture of academic complacency or the demolition of the meritocracy. It can be fulfilling for kids to chase a ribbon， as long as it's a ribbon the child really wants. And the very act of making that effort can bring out the best in anyone's work. But we cheat ourselves， and worse， we cheat our kids， if we view life as a single straight-line race in which one one-hundredth of the competitors finish in the money and everyone else loses.We will all be better off if we recognize that there are a great many races of varying lengths and outcomes. The challenge for parents is to help their children find the one that's right for them.

**答案:A**

**【结构化解析】**
**考点剖析与关键信息:** 本题考查英语阅读理解中对作者观点与修辞逻辑的精准把握能力，核心在于识别‘impersonal’在特定语境下的语义指向——即‘缺乏个体化关照、脱离读者主观体验’；题干关键词为‘arbitrary classification of books’与‘impersonal’，意图检测考生能否基于上下文推断抽象形容词的情感/评价色彩及其逻辑依据。
**选项深度对比/得分点:** A. 正确。'It brings about surprises'对应原文隐含逻辑：任意分类（arbitrary）因其不可预测性而打破读者预期，这种‘意外性’恰恰体现其非人格化（impersonal）——因不预设、不迎合、不追踪任何具体读者，故结果具有偶然性与疏离感；B. 错误。'Fails to track readers’ habits'看似合理，但‘track habits’属行为描述，原文未提数据追踪或行为分析，属无中生有型干扰；C. 错误。'Ignores the content of books'曲解‘arbitrary’含义——任意分类未必无视内容（如按颜色、装帧分类仍可保留内容），且原文焦点在分类标准与读者关系，非内容本身；D. 错误。'Fails to consider reader's preferences'表面贴近，但‘consider preferences’暗示主动评估过程，而‘arbitrary’强调根本无此考量意图，程度弱于D，且D将‘impersonal’窄化为偏好忽视，忽略其更本质的‘去主体性’内涵，属以偏概全型干扰。
**考点溯源与理论依据:** 依据《中小学教师专业标准（试行）》‘学科知识与教学能力’维度及《普通高中英语课程标准（2017年版2020年修订）》文化意识四级目标‘能辨析语言背后的价值取向与思维模式’；对应教育心理学中‘语境推理模型’（Graesser et al., 2003）：读者需激活背景知识（arbitrary→unplanned→unpredictable→impersonal）完成语义跃迁；同时关联Halliday系统功能语法中‘人际功能’理论——‘impersonal’在此体现为削弱作者-读者互动，消解评价主体，属典型的元话语标记考查。
